# Two reconstruction targets: a registration image and a truth image

**Status:** PROPOSAL (Claude, session 13, 2026-09-27) from Hooman's idea after the PF investigation (F86–F89,
2steve/08). Not decided. Next session's plan is at the end.

## Hooman's idea (as I understood it)
Two consumers, two different optima:
- **Radiographic video / registration** — sharpness, PSF, interior lung structure to hold on to; a *balanced*
  undersampling artefact is acceptable, even useful. Registration needs anchors, not amplitudes.
- **Physiological maps** — SNR and truth: unbiased amplitude, aliasing kept out of the lung, not just amplitude
  above thermal noise (both matter).
- The motion fields estimated on the first are applied to the second. So we know *where* we care about
  sharpness (the deformation estimate) and where we care about truth (the quantities being deformed).
- If PF's only sin is a fake edge, maybe that is fine as a registration anchor — CLAHE and gradient images were
  used as anchors before, and those do not invent content either.

## My take
1. **The split is right, and half of it already exists.** `gas_phase_magnitude` (2steve/06) was added for the
   videos because `real(F·b)` attenuates the moving rim; PCA registers gas, the maps are dissolved. Naming the two
   outputs R (registration) and T (truth) and optimising them separately is the clean version of that. The same
   gridded k-space feeds both, so R is a second apodisation/kernel setting on the same run, not a second recon.
2. **What R may contain and what it may not.** An anchor must be (a) locked to the anatomy and (b) *consistent
   from bin to bin*, so that the registration follows the lung and not the artefact. Deterministic per-bin
   transforms (CLAHE, gradients, unsharp mask) satisfy both: they add no k-space content, they only re-map what
   each bin already has. PF's ghost satisfies (a) — it is |F|-modulated — but I expect it to fail (b): it is
   generated by each bin's own hole pattern (different interleave sets per bin) and by noise outside the crop
   (independent per bin), and with matched noise its in-lung error grew from 7 % to 10–17 % (F89), i.e. the texture
   is largely noise-driven. A fake edge that moves between bins is worse than no edge. This is a prediction, not a
   measurement — it is cheap to measure (below), and Hooman's intuition deserves the number before the idea is
   dropped.
3. **Where "balanced undersampling artefact" lives.** Two knobs set it: `bindist0sq` (soft-bin width: wider bins =
   fewer holes, more temporal blur) and `nbins`. For R the criterion is registration quality (PCA's benchmark, C19/C20
   scorers); for T it is per-bin aliasing inside the lung (the truth-resampling harness measures exactly that:
   P1 loses 1–8 % of lung mean to holes depending on the object's spectrum). The two optima need not coincide —
   R can use wider soft bins for stability, T narrower ones for less blur, both from the same data.
4. **gplb is the other R/T knob.** 300 is one guess. For T the SNR-optimal apodisation is the matched filter = the
   FID's own envelope (measurable per session); for R a lighter apodisation (sharper PSF, more noise) is probably
   better because registration tolerates noise far better than bias. Same for the kernel rolloff (2steve/03):
   deapodise R, leave T Tyger-exact unless the maps are ratioed.
5. **What must not leak.** T stays linear and unbiased: `real(F·b)`, matched-filter apodisation, no sharpening, no
   PF, calcb as is. Anything that helps R by inventing content stays on the R side, and the deformation field is
   the only thing that crosses.
6. **Dead knobs.** Kernel dx, Kernel dt and Freq filter in the GUI are stored and passed to Tyger but never read;
   the real kernel widths are hardcoded (`kdist0sq 0.2`, `bxsz 2`, `bindist0sq 2`). Tell Steve (a line in 2steve).
